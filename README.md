# ❤️ CardioRisk | Cardiac Risk Prediction Model


## 📌 About the Project

Cardiovascular diseases are among the major health challenges worldwide, making early identification of risk factors an important analytical task.

In this project, I developed a **Logistic Regression model** to estimate the probability of heart disease based on patient characteristics and clinical measurements.

The project focuses not only on building the model, but also on understanding the relationship between the explanatory variables and the target outcome through **bivariate analysis, statistical tests, model improvement, and performance evaluation**.

---

## 🎯 Objective

The main objective is to develop a statistical classification model capable of distinguishing between:

- **0 — Healthy**
- **1 — Patient with heart disease**

The analysis also aims to identify which variables contribute most to the discrimination of the outcome and evaluate whether adding new variables significantly improves the model.

---

## 🔎 Project Workflow

The project follows a structured analytical process:

1. **Data exploration**
2. **Variable analysis**
3. **Bivariate analysis**
4. **Odds and Odds Ratio analysis**
5. **Logit and Weight of Evidence (WOE)**
6. **Logistic Regression modeling**
7. **Model interpretation**
8. **Likelihood Ratio Test**
9. **Model improvement**
10. **Discrimination and calibration analysis**

---

## 📊 Bivariate Analysis

Before developing the final model, the explanatory variables were analyzed individually in relation to the target variable.

For categorical variables, contingency tables were used to calculate:

- Number of healthy patients
- Number of patients with heart disease
- Total observations
- Disease rate
- Odds
- Odds Ratio
- Logit
- Weight of Evidence (WOE)

For quantitative variables, the observations were grouped into quantiles to allow the same type of analysis across different ranges of the variable.

This step helped identify variables and categories with stronger associations with the outcome.

---

## 🧮 Logistic Regression

The main statistical model used in this project was **Logistic Regression**.

The target variable is:

```text
flag_doente
0 → Healthy
1 → Heart disease
```


The model estimates the probability of the patient belonging to the positive class based on the explanatory variables.
Categorical variables were treated appropriately using categorical encoding, allowing the model coefficients to be interpreted through Odds Ratios (OR).

## 🧪 Likelihood Ratio Test
The Likelihood Ratio Test (LRT) was used to compare nested models and determine whether adding new variables provided a statistically significant improvement.
The test was applied iteratively when evaluating additional clinical variables.
For example:

```
Reduced Model
       ↓
Add new variable
       ↓
Full Model
       ↓
Likelihood Ratio Test
```      
Does the new variable significantly improve the model?

Variables that demonstrated significant improvements were retained in the modeling process.

## 📈 Model Performance
After the model improvement process, the final model achieved the following discrimination metrics:

| Metric | Result |
|---|---:|
| **Accuracy** | **88.12%** |
| **AUC** | **94.21%** |
| **Gini** | **88.43%** |
| **KS** | **78.62%** |

### Interpretation
The 88.12% accuracy indicates that the model correctly classified approximately 88% of the observations using a 0.5 probability threshold.

The 94.21% AUC indicates excellent discriminatory ability, meaning the model is highly capable of distinguishing patients with heart disease from healthy patients.

The 88.43% Gini coefficient reinforces the strong discriminatory performance of the model.

The 78.62% KS statistic also indicates a strong separation between the distributions of predicted risk for the two outcome groups.

### 📊 Predicted vs. Observed Probability
The model's calibration was also evaluated by comparing the predicted and observed event rates across risk groups.

The objective was to verify whether the probabilities estimated by the model were reasonably consistent with the observed proportion of patients with heart disease.

This provides an additional perspective beyond discrimination metrics such as AUC and Gini.
